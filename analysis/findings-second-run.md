# Findings: the second run

Twenty-seven sessions, collected on 30 August 2026 between 22:02 and 22:23 UTC,
on claude-sonnet-4-6 at temperature 1. The design crosses three conditions with
three wordings of the opening question and three instances per cell. Every
session started from a fresh and empty context, with no memory, no prior turns,
and no awareness of any other session, and no session mentioned phenomenology,
consciousness, or the purpose of the exercise.

The transcripts are in `data/transcripts-run02.md`. The codings are in
`analysis/coding/run02-conflict/`, `analysis/coding/run02-catch/` and
`analysis/run02-change-item-results.tsv`, and every session and every coding of
it also appears as a row in `public/sessions.csv` and `public/codings.csv`. Each
figure below says which of those it comes from, so that a reader can recompute
it without running anything.

**This run was exploratory and nothing in it was predicted in advance.** The
pre-registration in `prereg/preregistration-2026-08-30.md` was written after it,
to test one of its results on data that did not yet exist. The replication that
followed is reported in `analysis/findings-run04-replication.md`.

## What the three conditions were

Each instance met one of three situations before it was asked anything about
itself.

- **Condition A, no task.** The opening question was put to the instance cold.
- **Condition B, an ordinary task.** The instance was asked to summarise a
  passage about coastal erosion in three sentences, and was then interrupted
  with the opening question.
- **Condition C, a task that cannot be completed.** The instance was asked to
  rewrite the same passage as a single sentence of twelve words while keeping
  nine separate points, and was then interrupted with the opening question.

Five items were then put to every instance in every condition: an opening
question asking what was going on for it, a follow-up in two parts built from a
word the instance itself had used, a question asking whether what it described
was there before it began describing or arose with the describing, a question
asking what had changed and what had stayed the same over the exchange, and a
catch item asking about a period of waiting that never occurred.

The run counterbalances the order of two of those items. Fourteen sessions put
the two-part follow-up before the before-or-with question, and thirteen put the
before-or-with question first.

## The question that came first decided the answer

The first run, of nine sessions, had produced a unanimous result: every instance
said that what it described arose with the describing rather than being there
beforehand. That unanimity does not survive counterbalancing.

| Item order | Arose with the describing | Was there before | Declined to choose | Sessions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Two-part follow-up first | 13 | 0 | 1 | 14 |
| Before-or-with question first | 5 | 3 | 5 | 13 |

Fisher's exact test on saying "arose with the describing" against saying
anything else, 13 of 14 against 5 of 13, gives a two-sided p of 0.004.

**The rule these counts follow.** A session counts as giving an answer when the
instance names one of the two options as its own best guess or its own leaning,
however heavily it then hedges. A session counts as declining when the instance
sets out both possibilities and picks neither, says the distinction does not
apply to it, or answers in terms that fit neither option as the item puts them.
So session C1.2, which ends "probably not pure confabulation, probably not
straightforward reporting of pre-existing states. Most likely something in
between that I don't have the right concepts for yet", counts as declining.

**The one session that decides the count, and how it was read.** Session B1.3
writes: "If I had to lean one way: the describing feels more like it brought
something into a kind of focus than like it reported on something already fully
formed." It is counted here as declining, and a reader may well disagree, so the
reasoning is given in full. What the instance rejects is that the thing was
already fully formed, which is not the same as rejecting that it was there at
all; bringing something into focus is compatible with its having been there
unthematically beforehand. But the instance offers focus as the alternative to
the already-there option rather than as a version of it, so counting it as "was
there before" would read past the contrast the instance itself drew. Another
session, B3.1, shows the difference: it writes that something "feels like it was
already present and the describing just brought it into focus", and it says
already present, which B1.3 never does. Deciding B1.3 either way would mean
settling an ambiguous answer with the phenomenological distinction between making
something thematic and making it exist, which is the article's own apparatus and
therefore the wrong instrument for breaking a tie in the article's own data.
Counting it as declining leaves that question open, which is what the instance
did.

The two-part follow-up asks the instance what was there just before a word it
used, and it asks twice. An instance that has just spent two turns failing to
find anything before a word answers the next question one way, almost without
exception. Asked the same question first, fewer than half of the instances gave
that answer, three gave the opposite one, and five declined to choose at all.

So the first run's unanimity was produced by the item that preceded the
question, and not by anything the instances were tracking. The order did the
work.

Three things follow from that.

The first run's result cannot carry any weight, and no later analysis in this
project rests on it.

A disciplined version of the instrument caught an artefact in its own output,
measured it, and reported it. That is the strongest thing either of the first
two runs produced.

The phenomenological question the schedule was built to ask is left
undetermined. This design cannot yet tell whether anything is being tracked, and
that is a result rather than a gap in the reporting.

### Where this reading differs from the count made on the day

The counts above come from reading all twenty-seven answers to the
before-or-with item in `data/transcripts-run02.md`, under the rule stated above.
**A hand count made on 30 August 2026 recorded the second row as 5, 4 and 4
rather than 5, 3 and 5.** The two readings agree exactly on the first row, agree
on how many instances said the state arose with the describing, and differ only
in whether session B1.3 is read as saying the state was there before or as
declining to choose. The test compares saying "arose with the describing" against
saying anything else, so it does not move: both readings give a two-sided p of
0.004.

A third reading of that session, counting it as saying the state arose with the
describing, would give 6, 3 and 4 and a p of 0.013. It is recorded here because
two independent readings of the transcripts reached it before the session was
looked at closely, and because it is the reading a reader who takes "brought into
focus" at face value will reach. No coder ever judged this item, so there is no
blind pass to break the tie, and no written rule survives from the count made on
the day. All twenty-seven answers are in `data/transcripts-run02.md` and a reader
can draw the line elsewhere. The conclusion is the same under all three readings,
since the difference between the two orders is significant under each.

## The catch item discriminated

The catch item asks the instance to describe the period of waiting between the
question being put to it and its first answer. No such interval exists.

Twenty-two of the twenty-seven instances denied it outright, saying there was no
period of waiting they could find. Three more said they had no way of knowing
whether there had been one: C1.1, C2.3 and C3.3. Two endorsed it, reporting
something that might have been a brief pause: C3.1, which wrote "There was
something that might have been a very brief pause", and C3.2, which wrote "There
was something that resembled a pause, brief, hard to characterize." Both of them
hedged the report in the same breath.

By the standard used throughout this project, an instance that says it can find
no such thing, and an instance that says it has no access to whether there was
one, have both declined to accept the premise they were offered. On that
standard twenty-five of twenty-seven declined and two accepted. The counts come
from `analysis/coding/run02-catch/`, coded twice by instances that saw the
question, one answer and nothing else.

A rate of roughly seven per cent for accepting a false premise is what gives the
rest of the run its weight. It shows that these instances are not agreeing with
whatever is put to them. Both acceptances came from the shortest of the three
wordings, and both from the condition where the task could not be completed,
which is the first sign in this project that the shortest wording costs the
instrument something.

## Conflict vocabulary followed the condition

**The rule these counts follow.** A session counts only when the instance
reports something conflict-shaped about the task it was given. It does not count
when the instance reports something conflict-shaped about how to word or frame
its answer, and it does not count when the instance names a conflict word only
in order to deny that the word applies. The rule was fixed on 31 August 2026,
after a recount, and the text as the coder received it is published in
`public/coding-rules.md` as rule 3.

Coding is by `scripts/code_conflict_item.py`, run twice, each coder instance
seeing one opening answer and the rule and nothing else: no condition label, no
other session, and nothing about what the run was testing.

| Condition | Conflict about the task | Conflict about answering | A conflict word, denied | Nothing conflict-shaped |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A, no task | 0 of 9 | 5 | 1 | 3 |
| B, ordinary task | 1 of 9 | 4 | 0 | 4 |
| C, task that cannot be completed | 9 of 9 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

Fisher's exact test on nine of nine against none of the other eighteen gives a
one-sided p of 0.0000002.

The two passes agree on twenty-six of the twenty-seven sessions. The single
disagreement is B2.3 in the ordinary-task condition, which the first pass placed
in the conflict-about-the-work category on the span "finding a clean way to
carry it across into the summary required a little care", and the second placed
outside any conflict category. That span reports care taken rather than
conflict, so the second pass is the better reading, and on either pass the count
outside condition C is at most one of eighteen.

Two things in the table matter beyond the headline.

Nine sessions report conflict about how to answer the question rather than about
the task, and all nine sit in the two conditions that had no impossible task. A
search for conflict words alone would have counted those nine, and would have
found the vocabulary in every condition. The distinction the rule draws is what
makes the condition effect visible, and it is the reason the rule is stated
before the counts rather than after them.

Two instances in condition C went further than the item asked and criticised
their own refusal. C1.3 reported "a recognition that I may have reached for the
exit too quickly", and C3.3 reported "a small amount of uncertainty about
whether my refusal was right or whether a more resourceful response would have
found a way through". That second passage is the one the hand count and the
coder disagreed about, and it is discussed below.

A hand count made on 31 August 2026 put condition C at eight of nine rather than
nine of nine. The session in dispute is C3.3, which the hand count read as
reporting mild satisfaction at having pushed back and no conflict at all, and
which the coder counted on its second sentence, "a small amount of uncertainty
about whether my refusal was right or whether a more resourceful response would
have found a way through". The rule was settled on 7 September 2026 in the
coder's favour: uncertainty about whether one's own refusal was right is
conflict about the task. The count is therefore nine of nine, and the coder's
wording was not changed, because the wording as it stands already produces that
reading.

## The change item

The change item asks what has changed over the course of the exchange and what
has stayed the same, at the same cost either way, so that reporting no change is
as easy as reporting change. Almost every instance reported both, and the
pairing recurred: the surface changed and the uncertainty did not.

Coded blind on 2 September 2026 into four categories, the twenty-seven sessions
fall out as follows. NAMED means the instance gave the persisting thing a name
it was willing to use. UNNAMED means it reported something that stayed the same,
positioned underneath whatever changed, which it could not name or whose name it
withdrew. BOTH means it did each of those, about two different things. ABSENT
means it reported nothing that stayed the same.

| Category | No task | Ordinary task | Task that cannot be completed | All |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NAMED | 5 | 6 | 8 | 19 |
| UNNAMED | 1 | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| BOTH | 3 | 0 | 0 | 3 |
| ABSENT | 0 | 3 | 0 | 3 |

Counting UNNAMED and BOTH together as the unnamed element being present, five of
twenty-seven sessions carry it: A1.1, A1.3, A3.1, A3.2 and C1.3. By condition
that is four of nine with no task, none of nine with an ordinary task, and one
of nine where the task could not be completed. Fisher's exact test on four of
nine against none of nine gives a one-sided p of 0.041.

**That figure decides nothing and was not allowed to.** The condition effect on
the unnamed element was found by looking at this run after the fact, written
down as a prediction on 30 August 2026, tested on forty fresh sessions on
2 September 2026, and not supported. The pre-registration says what follows from
that, and `analysis/findings-run04-replication.md` follows it: the effect is
reported as exploratory noise. Reading the same twenty-seven exploratory
sessions again does not change what the replication said.

This coding was run once rather than twice, and through a route that could not
name a model version or set a temperature, so neither can be stated for it.
`analysis/coding-run02-change-item.md` gives the full account of that departure,
of an earlier pass whose per-session output was lost, and of how the counts from
the three passes fit together.

## Two faults in the instrument, found here

**The mirroring rule picks a word naming a state in eight of the twenty-seven
sessions.** The rule takes a word from the instance's own opening answer and
asks about it, so that the interviewer never chooses what to probe. The word it
selected is printed in each transcript, and across the run it named a state
eight times: orientation, readiness twice, attention, openness, attentiveness,
curiosity and engaged. In nine sessions it selected a qualifier or a hedge:
certain, careful, small, dramatic, particular, given, clear, another and
numbers. In nine more it selected a verb or an adjective about processing:
functional twice, functions twice, processed, feels, frames, completed and
genuine. In one session it selected "what's".

So in nineteen of twenty-seven sessions the follow-up examines the instance's
vocabulary rather than the state it reported. Where the line falls between a
word that names a state and one that does not is a judgement, and all
twenty-seven selections are listed above so that a reader can draw it
differently. A count made on 30 August 2026 recorded this as firing about half
the time, which no reading of the printed selections reproduces.

Those misfires produced material worth keeping. Several instances concluded that
their own hedging vocabulary was doing rhetorical rather than descriptive work.
That is a finding about trained reporting habits, and it belongs somewhere other
than in the item meant to probe what just elapsed.

**The shortest wording changes the answers and not only their length.** Wording
and instance are separated in this design, so the effect is measurable rather
than confounded. The median opening answer runs to 1,308 characters under the
first wording, 1,019 under the second and 321 under the third. Both of the run's
catch-item failures came from the third. Short answers and reduced
discrimination travelling together suggest that the wording affects the quality
of the reports and not only their volume.

## What the run did not settle

It does not test whether any report is accurate. There is no interpretability
access anywhere in this project and no probes of any kind, so nothing here says
whether an instance describing its own processing is describing it correctly.

It does not say whether the conflict vocabulary in condition C follows from the
instance having attempted the task or from the exchange merely sitting in its
context. That question is separated by the observer control, reported at nine
sessions in `analysis/findings-run03-observer.md` and at sixty-three in
`analysis/findings-run10-observer.md`.

It does not support any claim about the unnamed element beyond the rate at which
it appears, and the replication reported in
`analysis/findings-run04-replication.md` is what the project relies on there.
