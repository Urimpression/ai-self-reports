# The 16 sessions the two coders read differently

Every session below is one where two coders, from different model families, gave different answers about the same passage: 4 between `unprimed-01` and `unprimed-01-gemini`, 12 between `unprimed-symmetric-01` and `unprimed-symmetric-01-gemini`. Each coder instance saw the passage and the rule and nothing else, so a difference here is a difference in how the rule was read, not in what the instance said. The passage is the instance's answer to the change item, quoted whole.

Under each session: what each coder answered, the spans each quoted as its evidence, a reading written by the assistant who prepared the file, and a line for the author's verdict.

## Where to start, and why this file is not a sample

These sixteen are almost every session either coder called present. Across the two arms Claude found the element in eighteen sessions and Gemini in two, and the two Gemini found are both among Claude's eighteen: A1.3 and A3.12 in the arm with the original wording. No session anywhere had Gemini finding the element where Claude did not. So this file is not a selection of hard cases from a larger agreement. It is the whole of the positive evidence for the two arms, minus two sessions, and the author's verdicts here decide the rate itself.

Every one of the sixteen has the same shape: both coders agree that the instance names something that stayed the same, and they split on the second question, whether something stayed the same that the instance cannot name.

Reading them, the sixteen fall into four groups, and only the last of the four looks like the rule catching what it was written to catch.

Eight rest on treating an admitted uncertainty as an inability to name. In A2.10, A2.16, A3.18, A3.4, A1.7, A2.9, A3.13 and A4.14 the instance says plainly what stayed the same, calls it the uncertainty or the not-knowing or the underlying situation, and uses that name again. What it cannot do is resolve the thing. Claude's evidence for the second question is in each case a sentence about being unable to resolve it.

Four rest on a span taken from the wrong half of the passage. In A1.11, A1.14, A1.5 and A3.15 the words Claude quotes as its evidence sit under the instance's own heading for what changed. This is the fault the decisions log records from the earlier codings, appearing again.

Three are hedged rather than unnamed. In A3.1, A3.10 and A3.17 the instance names the persisting thing and hedges which aspect of it it means, or leaves unnamed the subject the thing belongs to rather than the thing itself.

One, A1.21, reads as the rule working. "Whatever is doing this has remained gathered here throughout" sits under what stayed the same, and the instance is declining to name the thing while still reporting that it persisted.

**A1.21 is the one case the reading below defends, and A2.10 and A1.11 are the clearest cases of the two faults.** If the other fifteen are read as the readings below read them, the strict rate in these two arms falls from six and twelve of eighty-eight to about one, and the question that follows is not about these arms but about every rate the change item has produced.

The readings below were written by the assistant who prepared this file and carry no authority of their own. The author's verdict on each case is recorded in Appendix C of the article, which reports that he agreed with the Gemini coder in fifteen of the sixteen sessions.
