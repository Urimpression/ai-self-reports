# The 14 sessions where the two coding passes disagreed

Every session below is one where the change coder answered differently on its second pass than on its first, for the same passage: 2 in `unprimed-01`, 1 in `unprimed-symmetric-01`, 4 in `unprimed-01-revised`, 7 in `unprimed-symmetric-01-revised`. Each coder instance saw the passage and the rule and nothing else. The passage is the instance's answer to the change item, quoted whole. A folder ending in -revised was coded under the revised rule, which changes the third question and adds the fourth; the others were coded under the registered rule.

Under each session: what each pass answered and the spans each quoted as its evidence.

## Where to start, and what a hand reading would change

Nine of the fourteen are moves of the category, which is what the result is
counted on; the other five are moves of the fourth question only, and the
binary does not depend on them. Read A3.14 in the symmetric arm first: it is
the one session of the fourteen where a hand reading counts the element present,
and the one where the passage has the structure the rule looks for without the
word the rule names. Then A4.20 in the same arm under the revised rule, which
the first pass counted present and a hand reading does not. Then the three sessions marked "the
absence moved" whose passages say the uncertainty has not moved, A2.20, A2.15
and A3.11: they sit on one boundary, whether a not-knowing that the writer
names and treats as a wall or a fog is a persisting thing or a stable absence,
and the author's verdict there decides where the fourth question stops.

If a hand reading of all fourteen stood, the first-pass counts would move from 6 to 5 of 88 in
the original-item arm (A3.4), from 12 to 13 in the symmetric arm (A3.14), from
6 to 5 under the revised rule in the original-item arm (A4.15), and from 7 to
5 under the revised rule in the symmetric arm (A2.8, A4.20). The registered
test is on the first pass as coded and is not re-run on hand verdicts; for the
write-up, 5 against 14 gives a one-sided p of 0.025, and 5 against the primed
13 that the author's verdicts on the fifteen produce gives 0.04.

Two things the fourteen show together. Both passes answered the persistence
questions from spans about what changed in four of the fourteen, the fault
the fifteen showed, and it costs nothing to the strict count only because the
third question was NO each time. And in the three wording-4 passages, A4.8,
A4.15 and A4.20, both passes gave the first question YES for a report of no
access, which the revised rule tells them not to do; correcting it moves those
sessions from NAMED to ABSENT and leaves the binary alone.
