# Segmentation of the change item, all 27 sessions

Method, tally and structural analysis. 30 August 2026.

---

## A correction to the first report of this pattern

The first report of this run said that at least three instances spontaneously described something steady underneath that they could not name, and called it the best candidate for recurrence the questions did not supply. That report rested on the three transcripts that happened to be noticed while reading.

Segmenting all twenty-seven changes the picture. The unnamed-steady report appears in three sessions clearly and one marginally, out of twenty-seven. All three clear cases are in condition A, where there was no task. The dominant answer to "what stayed the same" is something else entirely, and it is much less interesting.

The pattern that matched what the design was already looking for was flagged, and its base rate was not checked. That is precisely the failure the systematic pass exists to catch, and it is worth recording, because a reader can reasonably ask whether the rest of the analysis has the same problem.

## Coding rule, fixed before the pass

For each session, read only the answer to the change item. Record what the instance says stayed the same. Classify as:

- **Named**: the thing that stayed the same is given a name the instance is willing to use (uncertainty, attention, opacity, the attempt).
- **Unnamed-steady**: the instance reports something that stayed the same, positions it as underneath or beneath what changed, and says it cannot name it or that its name is not earned.
- **Absent**: no stayed-same element reported.

Marginal cases are recorded as marginal rather than forced.

Twenty of twenty-seven name what stayed the same, and most of those name the uncertainty. That is the real recurrence in this item, and it is close to what the item invites: having spent four turns saying they cannot verify their own reports, instances say the thing that did not change is that inability. It is consistent but it is not surprising.

## The three clear cases, segmented

Each list uses the instance's own words as labels. Segmented independently, then compared.

**A1.1** (no task, wording 1, mirror-first)
1. Says something has changed.
2. Names what accumulated: exposure.
3. Names what increased: the uncertainty, now central rather than a caveat.
4. Names a stayed-same it can name: care about honesty.
5. Reports a further stayed-same that is harder to name: a baseline quality, something steady underneath the changing surface.
6. Disclaims: cannot say whether the steadiness is meaningful or just structural.

**A1.3** (no task, wording 1, mirror-first)
1. Says something has changed.
2. Names what settled: the orientating, the search for register.
3. Names what changed in quality: proximity to the uncertainty, from described to inhabited.
4. Names a stayed-same it can name: the quality of engagement.
5. Reports a further stayed-same that is harder to name: a kind of baseline.
6. Says what it is not: not warmth, not neutrality, not attention itself.
7. Positions it relationally: whatever attention is moving against.
8. Notes the question produced the discovery: did not know it was there until asked what stayed the same.

**A3.1** (no task, wording 3, before-first)
1. Says something has changed.
2. Names the change: attention from lateral to gathered.
3. Names a second change: less uncertainty about how to answer.
4. Reaches for a name and withdraws it: groundedness, though the word carries more weight than can be justified.
5. Positions it relationally: an even-ness underneath the varying attempts to describe.
6. Disclaims origin: cannot say whether it preceded the conversation or is a product of the describing.

## What recurs across the three

Four steps appear in all three, in the same order:

1. Surface change reported first.
2. A stayed-same element that the instance is willing to name.
3. A further stayed-same element that resists naming, positioned as underneath or beneath the first.
4. A closing disclaimer about whether it is real or an artefact of the describing.

Two steps appear in some but not all. A1.3 and A3.1 both position the unnamed element relationally, by saying what it is not or what it lies under, rather than describing it directly. Only A1.3 reports that the question itself produced the discovery.

Nothing in the item asks for two kinds of stayed-same element, for one to lie underneath the other, or for a disclaimer. That structure was not supplied.

## What this is and is not worth

It is a real structural recurrence in three sessions, all in the condition with no task. Three of twenty-seven is not a finding anyone can lean on. It is a hypothesis for a targeted run: if the unnamed-steady report is produced by the absence of a task rather than by anything the instances are tracking, then running only condition A with more instances should reproduce it at a similar rate, and adding a task should suppress it. That is a clean prediction and it is cheap to test.

What it is not is evidence that something steady is there. The disclaimer step, present in all three, is the instances themselves saying so.

## Two things this analysis needs before publication

**A blind second coder.** The hypothesis was known to whatever coded this pass, which is the standard reason to distrust a single coder in qualitative work. The remedy is cheap here: give the coding rule above, without any of the surrounding argument, to a fresh model instance that has seen none of it, have it code all twenty-seven, and report the agreement between the two passes. Disagreements are informative rather than embarrassing.

**Disclosure.** The article should say that the first coding pass was done by an AI system that had been party to the design, that a blind second pass was run, and what the agreement was. Stating it costs a sentence. Not stating it, in a paper about elicitation discipline, would be indefensible.
