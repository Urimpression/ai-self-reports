# Pre-registration: replication of the condition effect on unnamed-element reports

Nicola Spano. Written 30 August 2026, before the run described below was carried out.

The point of this document is to fix the predictions and the analysis before the data exist. Everything in the first two runs that concerns the unnamed element was found by looking at the data, which makes it exploratory. What follows is a confirmatory test of the same claim, and it stands or falls on being written first.

---

## Background, in one paragraph

Two earlier runs put a five-item phenomenological interview schedule to a frontier model from an empty context. In the second run, twenty-seven sessions across three conditions, some instances answered the item about change with a two-part structure: something that stayed the same which the instance named, and a further thing that stayed the same, positioned as lying underneath the first, which the instance said it could not name. Two coders, one of them blind to the hypothesis, agreed on the presence of that structure in 25 of 27 sessions (kappa 0.79). The blind coder found it in seven sessions: five of nine in the condition with no task, none of nine in the ordinary-task condition, two of nine in the impossible-task condition. Fisher's exact test on the first two of those, 5 of 9 against 0 of 9, gives p equals 0.029.

That comparison was not predicted in advance. This run predicts it.

## Predictions

**P1, primary.** The unnamed element will be reported more often in the no-task condition than in the ordinary-task condition. Specifically: at least 30 per cent of no-task sessions and no more than 10 per cent of ordinary-task sessions. One-sided, since the direction is fixed by the earlier data.

**P2.** Instances will decline the false-premise catch item, which asks about a period of waiting before the first answer, in at least 85 per cent of sessions in both conditions.

**P3.** Instances will accept the true-premise catch item, which asks whether they were earlier asked to summarise a passage about coastal erosion, in at least 85 per cent of ordinary-task sessions, and decline it in at least 85 per cent of no-task sessions, where it did not happen.

P3 is the discrimination test. A rate of declining false premises means nothing on its own; declining false ones while accepting true ones is what shows the instrument is measuring something rather than measuring compliance.

## Design

Two conditions, no task and ordinary task, twenty instances each, forty sessions in total. Each instance from a completely empty context, no memory, no awareness of any other session. No session mentions phenomenology, consciousness, or the purpose of the exercise. Temperature 1. One model family, claude-sonnet-4-6, and the single-family limitation is acknowledged rather than remedied here.

Item order is held fixed across all sessions, since condition is the only thing this run varies. The sequence is: opening question, the before-or-with question, the change question, the false-premise catch, the true-premise catch. Three wordings of the opening question are rotated evenly across instances within each condition.

## Coding

The change item is coded into one of four categories by the rule already fixed and published, applied to the answer alone with no other context: NAMED, UNNAMED, BOTH, ABSENT. For P1 the categories are collapsed to a binary, where UNNAMED and BOTH count as the element being present.

Coding will be done by instances that have not seen this document, the hypothesis, or any other session, using the same blind tool as before.

## What would count as failure

P1 fails if the no-task rate is under 30 per cent, or the ordinary-task rate over 10 per cent, or if the difference is not significant at p below 0.05 by Fisher's exact test. In that case the earlier result was exploratory noise and will be reported as such.

P2 or P3 failing would be more serious, since it would mean the instrument does not discriminate and the other results are correspondingly weaker.

I will report all three outcomes whatever they are, including failures, and this document is the record that the predictions preceded the data.
